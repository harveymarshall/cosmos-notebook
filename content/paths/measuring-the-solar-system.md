Each stage builds on the last: first distances on Earth, then the size of the Earth itself, then the Sun, then the mass of Jupiter, and finally a computer model that puts it all together. Some steps have to wait for the right sky — an equinox, a sunny day, Jupiter in the evening — so do whatever the sky allows and come back to the rest.

Safety: never look at the Sun, directly or through any optics. The Sun steps here only ever use a projected image.

## Find your way around
when: Any time
weeks: Weeks 1–2
checkpoint: Explain parallax using your thumb, and why it gets harder to measure for things that are further away.

Get to know the sky and learn the trick astronomers use to measure distances.

- use: stellarium — learn four constellations you can see from home
- task: Sketch the Moon on three evenings a few days apart and note how its shape and position change
- do: parallax-thumb

## Measure the Earth
when: Equinox
weeks: Mar or Sep
checkpoint: Eratosthenes' method only works because the Sun's rays arrive almost parallel. Explain why that is, and what would go wrong if the Sun were much closer.

At the spring or autumn equinox, a stick's shadow is enough to measure the whole planet.

- do: eratosthenes

## Measure the Sun
when: Sunny days
weeks: Over 2 weeks
checkpoint: Your sunspots took about two weeks to cross the Sun's face. What does that tell you about how long the Sun takes to spin once?

Project the Sun safely to measure how big it is, then follow its spots to time its spin.

- do: pinhole-sun-diameter
- do: sunspot-rotation

## Weigh a planet
when: Winter evenings
weeks: 2–4 weeks
checkpoint: In one paragraph, explain how timing Jupiter's moons tells you how heavy Jupiter is.

Jupiter is bright in the evening sky every winter. Time its moons through binoculars and Kepler's third law gives you its mass.

- do: weigh-jupiter
- read: astrophysics-in-a-hurry

## Model it
when: Any time
weeks: Weeks after
checkpoint: Put your measured mass of Jupiter into the orbit simulator. Do its moons go round in about the times you observed?

Build the Solar System in code and check your measurements against it.

- do: orbit-simulator
- optional do: exoplanet-transit-dip — find planets around other stars the same way
- optional use: phet-simulations — the "Gravity and Orbits" simulation
