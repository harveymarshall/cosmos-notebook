Each stage pairs a little reading with a hands-on project. Your own progress shows up here and in [My notebook](/notebook/).

## Get oriented
id: orient
when: Late Oct
weeks: Weeks 1–2

A short, beautiful overview of modern physics, one easy indoor experiment, and learning your way around the night sky.

- read: seven-brief-lessons
- do: cd-spectroscope
- use: stellarium — learn 3–4 constellations
- task: Pick a podcast for the commute

## Light and quantum basics
id: light
when: Nov
weeks: Weeks 3–6

What light actually is: measure its speed, then see it behave like a wave with nothing more than a laser pointer.

- read: quantum-guide-perplexed
- do: speed-of-light-chocolate
- do: laser-double-slit
- task: Watch a short video on wave–particle duality before the double-slit

## Your first fundamental constant
id: constant
when: Late Nov – mid Dec
weeks: Weeks 7–9

Use coloured LEDs and a multimeter to measure Planck's constant — the number at the heart of quantum physics.

- do: planck-constant-leds
- task: Order a cheap multimeter and LED kit early

## Into space
id: space
when: Mid Dec – Jan
weeks: Weeks 10–15

Jupiter is in the evening sky all winter: track its moons on weekend nights and use Kepler's laws to weigh the planet.

- read: astrophysics-in-a-hurry
- do: weigh-jupiter
- do: sunspot-rotation
- task: Observe on Friday and Saturday nights
- task: Sunspot sketches on clear weekend days

## Level up
id: level-up
when: Feb – Mar
weeks: Weeks 16–22

Measure the size of the Earth at the spring equinox, then start simulating physics in code.

- do: eratosthenes
- do: orbit-simulator
- use: phet-simulations
- optional read: theoretical-minimum — Susskind's free lectures follow the book
