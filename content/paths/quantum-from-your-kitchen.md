Is light a wave or a stream of particles? By the end of this path you'll have seen it behave as both, and measured the two numbers that tie those pictures together: the speed of light, *c*, and Planck's constant, *h*.

Safety: never point a laser pointer at anyone's eyes, and never look into the beam or its reflection off something shiny.

## What is light?
when: Weeks 1–2
checkpoint: Your microwave's frequency is printed on a label on the back (usually 2,450 MHz). Explain to a friend how two melted spots half a wavelength apart give you the speed of light.

Start with the big picture, then measure the fastest thing there is using a bar of chocolate.

## Light is a wave
when: Weeks 3–5
checkpoint: Why do the bright bands from the double slit spread further apart when the gap between the slits gets narrower?

Split light into colours, then make it interfere with itself. Interference is the fingerprint of a wave.

- do: cd-spectroscope
- do: laser-double-slit
- use: phet-simulations — try the "Wave Interference" simulation before the real thing

## Light is also a particle
when: Weeks 6–10
checkpoint: A blue LED needs a higher voltage to switch on than a red one. Explain why, using the energy of a single photon, E = hf.

Light also arrives in lumps of energy called photons. Measure the size of those lumps with LEDs, then meet the strangest part of quantum mechanics: measuring something changes it.
