## The question

Can I measure the speed of light in my kitchen?

## Learn just enough first

- [ ] Look up what a standing wave is
- [ ] Learn the wave equation: speed = frequency × wavelength

## Kit

- [ ] Microwave oven (check the label on the back for its frequency — usually 2450 MHz)
- [ ] A large flat chocolate bar, or a plate of grated cheese or marshmallows
- [ ] A ruler
- [ ] A plate

## My prediction

The accepted value is about 300,000 km per second. How close do I think a kitchen ruler can get?

## Method

1. Take the turntable out so the food doesn't rotate.
2. Put the chocolate on an upside-down plate and heat it for about 15–20 seconds, until a few spots just start to melt.
3. Measure the distance between neighbouring melted spots, in metres.
4. Repeat with a fresh bar or a different spot, and average the results.

> **Safety:** Don't run the microwave empty, and take the plate out with oven gloves — it can get hot.

## Result

The melted spots are the hot points of a standing wave, which sit half a wavelength apart. So the wavelength is twice the spot distance \(d\), and:

$$ c = 2 \, d \, f $$

With \(f = 2.45 \times 10^{9}\) Hz, a spot spacing of about 6 cm gives roughly \(2.9 \times 10^{8}\) m/s. Compare my number with the accepted \(2.998 \times 10^{8}\) m/s.

## Explain it to a friend

Microwaves are light you can't see. Inside the oven they bounce back and forth and set up a pattern of hot and cold spots. Measure the spacing, multiply by the oven's frequency, and you've measured the speed of light.

## New questions

- Why are the hot spots exactly half a wavelength apart?
- What limits my accuracy most — the ruler, or deciding where a spot really is?
